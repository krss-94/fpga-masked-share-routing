01 · why any of this matters

A chip's power draw gives away what it's computing.

bit = 0 bit = 1

Same circuit, different bit → a slightly different power trace, every time. Watch enough traces and the secret falls out statistically. This is a side-channel.

02 · the defense

Split the secret. Neither half leaks anything alone.

secret → share A (pure noise) + share B (pure noise). Only when both meet does the secret exist again — and a safe design never lets that meeting happen anywhere probeable.

03 · the chip, from zero

An FPGA is a tiny city. Watch one block of it.

building A wants to reach building B. the light decides if it can.
tap the traffic light to flip it yourself
04 · where it breaks

Fenced apart. Wired together anyway.

Pblocks fence off placement. Nothing fences off routing — the wires between fenced regions can still cross the same junction.

06 · a separate chip, a separate question

Does the synthesized logic leak? A simulated proxy says: not yet, not here.

This is a different device and a different experiment line from the XCZU2 routing work above — a conventionally-synthesized 2-share AND gadget on XC7A100T (Artix-7), evaluated with simulated Welch's t-test (TVLA) leakage proxies, not the switchbox conflict analysis.

1.240
worst |t|, RTL simulation
1.280
worst |t|, gate-level simulation
4.5
TVLA threshold — not exceeded

Both figures are simulated TVLA proxies, not physical FPGA power or EM measurements — no silicon was measured. A follow-on second-order (pairwise) TVLA pass is currently on hold: the stimulus construction used to test share pairs forces a same-variable correlation, which confounds the result before it can be read as a leakage finding.

07 · what's actually established, right now

Structural evidence on routing. Simulated evidence on leakage. Not yet the same claim.

structural evidence — XCZU2 routing
Every avoidable cross-share switchbox conflict is closed (M3c, milestone chain M1 → M3 → M3b → M3c). Two conflicts remain, confirmed structural by two independent methods — this is routing-geometry evidence only, not a claim of physical leakage resistance.
simulation evidence — XC7A100T TVLA
First-order TVLA proxies (RTL and gate-level) stay under threshold, on the separate M4 / TVLA experiment line — a different device and a different implementation from the XCZU2 routing result above.
not yet linked
Same-lineage leakage validation of the XCZU2 M3c implementation is not currently established — the routing result and the TVLA result speak to two different builds, and are never combined into one statistic.
on hold / not established
Genuine second-order TVLA validation is on hold — the current stimulus construction confounds same-variable share pairs. No physical power/EM measurement has been performed on any device.
